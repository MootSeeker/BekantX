Before you start

This guide shows how to assemble the BekantX controller: the controller board, the OLED display, the keypad and the printed enclosure (revision r3), and how to mount the finished unit under the desktop.

Status of this design. The enclosure r3 has been printed and case and lid fit together (photos below). The fit of the controller board has only been checked in CAD: no collisions, and the insertion path is free. A complete assembly with a populated board has not been done yet – the board has not been manufactured. Steps that rest on unverified assumptions are marked with an orange ! box like this one.

Pictures marked Photo show the real printed part; pictures marked Render are renderings of the CAD models (colours are render defaults, not the filament).

The controller board at a glance

The board measures 129 × 38 × 1.6 mm. On its right short edge it has a small tab (3.8 × 11 mm) carrying the USB-C socket; this tab brings the socket flush with the side wall of the case.

Assembly

1

Insert the keypad

  1. Lay the case down with the open back facing up.
  2. Place the keypad from the inside: each cap goes into its hole in the front, the spine rests against the inside of the front wall.
  3. Seen from the front: M1, M2, M3, M4 on the left, the display window in the middle, Up and Down on the right.

This button mapping is an assumption and must match the firmware (BTN_1 … BTN_6).

2

Connect the display to the board

  1. Slide the display FPC into connector J3 on the front side of the board (contacts facing down towards the board) and close the latch.
  2. The display sits upside down for the viewer once it is in the case; the firmware rotates the image.

Check pin 1 before applying power. Its position was derived from the drawing in the display datasheet; the connector datasheet was not available. If the FPC is inserted the wrong way round, the supplies end up on signal pins. Best: print the footprint 1:1 and test-fit the real display.

3

Fit the board

  1. Put a piece of thin double-sided tape on the front of the display glass, outside the viewing area.
  2. Hold the board with its front side (buttons, display) down towards the front. The glass goes into the pocket behind the display window.
  3. Insertion path: lower the board offset by about 1.4 mm away from the USB side, so the tab with the USB socket passes through the cut-out in the rim. Then slide the board towards the USB side until the socket sits in the wall pocket, flush with the outside of the wall. The buttons now sit under the caps of the keypad.
  4. Fix the board on the bosses with six M2.5 × 6 screws. Hand-tight only – they cut their thread directly into the plastic.
  5. Quick check: all six caps can be pressed and spring back, the USB-C socket is reachable from outside.

The FPC folds between the edge of the glass and button SW5 on its way to the connector. This fold is not modelled in CAD: on the first assembly make sure nothing is kinked or pinched.

4

Flash the firmware (case still open)

The RST and BOOT buttons, the USB SEL switch (selects which controller is connected to the USB-C socket) and the SWD port are on the back side of the board. They can only be reached with the lid off, so flash the firmware before closing the lid.

5

Connect the desk cable

  1. Disconnect the desk from mains first.
  2. Lead the desk cable through the cable exit (9 × 5 mm) in the arm and plug it onto connector J10. The pinout is printed on the board: LIN, GND, VDESK.
  3. Secure the cable with a cable tie through the slots in the arm.

The desk's electrical interface has not been measured yet. The pinout of the desk connector, the supply voltage and the LIN levels are assumptions. The input protection is designed for continuous voltages up to 33 V. Before the first connection to a real desk, check the pinout with a multimeter. Cable diameter and plug have not been measured either.

6

Close the lid

  1. Hook the two tongues at the arm end of the lid into the pockets of the end wall.
  2. Swing the lid forward about this hinge until its front edge snaps in under the two lips of the front wall.
  3. Secure the lid with two M2.5 × 6 screws in the arm (the heads sit in counterbores).

The snap holds through a small elastic deflection of the lid (about 0.3 mm), so print accuracy makes it looser or tighter. If it is too tight, remove the "elephant's foot" of the first layer on the lid's nose.

Opening: undo the two lid screws, put a fingernail under the tab at the front edge (the front wall is chamfered underneath) and swing the lid back.

7

Mount under the desktop

The arm is angled at 55° and rests against the underside of the desktop; the front block with buttons and display faces forward. Screw the enclosure to the underside of the desktop with two countersunk wood screws through the two ears on the arm (hole 4.5 mm, countersink 8.5 mm). Choose the screw length so the screws cannot come through the desktop.
